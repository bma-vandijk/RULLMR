
Responsible use of Large Language Models in Research practices

About the course
Large language models (LLMs), such as ChatGPT and similar AI tools, are increasingly being used in research. This eight-session, practice-oriented course helps researchers use these tools critically, effectively, and responsibly throughout the research cycle.

Rather than focusing on a single tool, the course provides a practical framework for deciding when and how LLMs can add value to research, when their use is inappropriate, and how researchers can remain accountable for the quality, integrity, and traceability of their work.

Study load, 1.5 EC and Epi-B
The nominal completion route has a study load of approximately 42 hours, awards 1.5 EC, and counts towards the LUMC Epi-B programme. To qualify, participants must:

    complete one introductory session and at least five different theme sessions;
    participate actively and complete a short recovery task if required; and
    pass the individual final assignment with at least 55/100 and all four safety gates.

Participants who meet the attendance requirements without passing the final assignment receive an attendance-only certificate. This certificate does not award EC and does not count towards Epi-B.

Target group
The course is intended for all research staff, including PhD candidates, postdoctoral researchers, research assistants, data analysts, and senior researchers. It is relevant to researchers from all disciplines, whether they are new to LLMs or already have some experience using them.

What will you learn?
By the end of the course, participants will be able to:

    explain in practical terms how large language models generate responses, what their strengths and limitations are, and why the same question can produce different answers;
    decide whether an LLM is appropriate for a research task by considering the sensitivity of the data, the level of human expertise required, and the potential risks;
    choose an appropriate LLM environment for their work, use it safely within institutional policies, and recognise when an alternative approach is needed;
    use an LLM as part of a structured research workflow, document how it was used, and ensure that the work can be understood and reproduced by others;
    critically evaluate AI-generated output for accuracy, completeness, reproducibility, privacy, confidentiality, bias, copyright, and ethical acceptability, and decide whether it should be accepted, revised, or rejected; and
    improve an AI-supported workflow through testing and feedback, communicate its limitations transparently, and take final responsibility for all scientific decisions and outputs.

Course content and format
The introductory session covers how LLMs work, the different ways in which they can be used, and their main limitations and risks.

The following sessions explore the responsible use of LLMs in key stages of the research cycle:

    literature searching and reviewing;
    research questions and study design;
    data collection and data management;
    data analysis and interpretation;
    scientific writing and communication;
    peer review and editorial tasks; and
    grant applications and research proposals.

Each session combines individual preparation, discussion with a fellow participant, group discussion, practical exercises, and reflection. Participants will work with realistic research scenarios and consider how the methods and safeguards discussed can be applied in their own research context.

Throughout the course, emphasis is placed on safe and purposeful use of LLMs, careful checking of outputs, transparent documentation, and meaningful human oversight. The course encourages participants to use LLMs as supportive tools rather than as substitutes for scientific judgement, methodological expertise, or researcher responsibility.

Final assignment
The course concludes with an individual assignment in which participants create or improve a scientific product using an LLM-supported workflow. They will critically evaluate the process and outcome, reflect on the benefits and limitations of LLM use, and formulate practical standards for responsible use in their own work.

A score of at least 55/100 and passing all four safety gates are required for the 1.5 EC and Epi-B completion route.
